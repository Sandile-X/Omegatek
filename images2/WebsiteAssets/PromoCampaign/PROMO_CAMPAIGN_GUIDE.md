## Usage notes

- Use `01` as the flagship website image. It has deliberate left-side space for the headline and CTA.
- Use `03` without adding more copy over the artwork.
- Use `06` as the strongest square social asset.
- Use `07` beside Dynamic EQ copy; do not cover the controls with text.
- Use `08` when the surrounding page is white or visually restrained.
- Keep the original screenshots in the parent `WebsiteAssets` folder for factual product galleries and documentation. The campaign images are stylised presentation art.

## Built-in image-generation prompt set

1. Luxury mastering chamber with acoustic architecture, cyan/violet spectral energy, product on the right and negative space on the left.
2. Digital-precision/analog-soul visual with the interface above a monumental vinyl groove and warm amber/cyan light.
3. Vertical social poster with the exact text “SHAPE WHAT MOVES”, an orbital EQ ribbon and the active product interface.
4. Technical exploded-view visual with separate translucent layers for EQ curve, pre/post spectra, dynamics and metering.
5. Photoreal mastering-suite editorial at night with the plugin as the clear hero on the main monitor.
6. Square product sculpture with eight colour-coded band nodes orbiting a black-glass pedestal.
7. Dynamic EQ macro emphasizing threshold, attack, release and ratio with controlled spectrum energy.
8. Bright Swiss-modern editorial scene for white-site placement, with translucent frequency ribbons and generous copy space.
9. Compact black-aluminium desktop mastering console with a wide spectrum display, walnut cheeks and illuminated physical controls.
10. Large-format studio desk integration with eight numbered hardware band modules and a dedicated Dynamic EQ strip.

The corrected `v2` hardware concepts follow the actual **Mastering: Vinyl-Style** state: Band 1 is a 35 Hz Low Cut, Band 2 is a +0.8 dB Low Shelf, Bands 4–6 attenuate by -0.5/-1/-2 dB, and Bands 3/7/8 are bypassed.

Across every prompt, the supplied active-use screenshot was the product reference; generated UI changes, additional controls, unrelated hardware branding, watermarks and generic cyberpunk clutter were explicitly excluded.
